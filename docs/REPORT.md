## 1. Performance and Amdahl's law

**Method.** The same simulation (20 days = 80 steps) at several population sizes; speedup is the wall time at 1 thread divided by the time at N threads of the same backend. An Amdahl fit gives the serial fraction s (speedup(N) = 1 / (s + (1-s)/N)). Contacts are agent-location visits. Apple M1 (4P+4E cores), 1M agents; 20 days (80 steps), 10 reps + 1 warm-up, median shown (mean, std dev and 95% CI of the mean are in the JSON), seed 42

| rayon dynamic | 1.47x | 2.03x | 2.24x | 0.351 | 2.85x |

**Analysis.** Speedup saturates well below the thread count; the fitted serial fraction is the share of the run that does not scale (it also absorbs memory-bandwidth limits and the efficiency cores, so it is not purely algorithmic). Best 8-thread speedup: OpenMP guided.

## 10. Calibration (rough)

**Method.** Grid search of the beta scale and asymptomatic fraction against the assumed target above, early growth fitted by log-linear regression on daily new infections; calibrated values live in config/calibrated.toml, selected with `--params calibrated`; defaults (and golden hashes) untouched. Details: docs/CALIBRATION.md.

Target (ASSUMED, illustrative, not a citation): early doubling time 5.0 days and asymptomatic share 0.4. Grid on 100,000 synthetic agents, 5 runs per point, base seed 42, 8 beta scales x 6 asymptomatic scales (coarse) + 11 x 6 (fine). Default parameters double in 12.1 days. Fitted: beta x1.6, P(asymptomatic) x1.1 (share 0.407), doubling 4.98 +/- 0.209 d (sd over runs).

| city | params | runs | R initial (first 7 d) | baseline attack rate | baseline peak A+I | isolation attack rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mumbai | default | 50 | 1.33 | 41.7% | 42,582 | 2.2% |
| mumbai | calibrated | 20 | 2.06 | 72.9% | 123,107 | 48.8% |
| bengaluru | calibrated | 20 | 1.96 | 71.2% | 108,691 | 37.9% |

Transfer to the real city graphs (1M agents, 5 seeds each): mumbai doubling 4.65 d (range 4.59-4.72); bengaluru doubling 5.51 d (range 5.4-5.56).

**Analysis.** What it shows: the model can reproduce an assumed 5-day doubling time with beta x1.6, and that fit transfers approximately (within the 4-6 day band) to the two city graphs even though it was fitted on synthetic cities. What it does not show: the target is an assumption, only growth rate was fitted (no deaths, hospital load or timing of a peak), the two parameters are only weakly identified by one growth rate, attack rates near 70% follow from the 5-day doubling and the model's SEIR structure, and nothing here is a forecast.

## Threats to validity

- **Thermal throttling.** The M1 is passively cooled and was not monitored with a die-temperature sensor (that needs sudo). Each Mac result records `pmset -g therm` before and after the run; none reported a thermal or performance warning, which rules out only a flagged throttle, not mild frequency drops. Long multi-thread runs on the 4P+4E chip may run slower than short ones; the sequential 1M run shows a wide interval (std dev 0.50 s on a 2.3 s median), a sign of such variation or background load.
- **Short runs.** Each timed run is 20 simulated days (80 steps), 0.02-3 s for the CPU backends; 10 repetitions after one warm-up. Confidence intervals describe run-to-run noise on one machine and session, not variation between days or boots; small differences (a few percent) between schedules or variants are inside that noise unless the CIs are disjoint.
- **Single machines.** Mac results are one Apple M1 (8 GB, 4P+4E cores); Kaggle results are one shared Xeon + T4 session, with 5 reps and no confidence intervals. Nothing here generalises to other CPUs, core counts or memory systems, and the Mac and Kaggle numbers are not comparable.
- **Core placement.** macOS gives no thread pinning, so a 'single-thread' run may land on a performance or an efficiency core. The 1M-element NEON timer kernel ranged 0.24-0.64 ns/element across 10 reps, and the same kernel measured 6.5x (scalar/NEON, median) in one roofline run and 2.9x in the next, and the 1-thread triad bandwidth was 25 GB/s in one run and 49 GB/s in the next. Single-thread kernel ratios and the roofs are therefore only indicative.
- **Other caveats.** The weak-scaling and MPI runs use synthetic worlds and a CPU with unequal cores, so efficiency below 1 mixes algorithmic and hardware effects. Roofline ops/bytes per element are static tallies, not hardware-counter measurements; the roofs come from simple loops and may be below the true peak. Each MPI point has 5 reps and large intervals.

## Limitations

- **Epidemic parameters are illustrative.** The built-in defaults are hand-tuned. config/calibrated.toml is a rough fit of beta and the asymptomatic fraction to an assumed doubling time (section 10); durations, severity and scenario effects are not fitted to any real outbreak, and no number here is a forecast.
- **Kaggle run settings.** Kaggle numbers come from a shared notebook: Tesla T4, 15360 MiB, 580.178.04, 7.5 (x2 listed), Intel(R) Xeon(R) CPU @ 2.00GHz with 4 cores, Linux-6.18.55+-x86_64-with-glibc2.39, run 2026-11-22T05:41:11Z. Benchmarks use 20 days (80 steps), 5 reps + 1 warm-up, seed 42, 100k and 1M agents only; the Xeon thread sweep is 1 and 4 threads. Other tenants may add noise.
- **ncu unavailable.** Nsight Compute could not read GPU counters (==ERROR== ERR_NVGPUCTRPERM - The user does not have permission to access NVIDIA GPU Performance Counters on the target device 0. For instructions on enabling pe), so there is no measured branch efficiency.
- OpenACC was attempted on Kaggle but no OpenACC-capable compiler (NVHPC) was available; no results.
- **AVX2 only on one shared Xeon.** NEON was measured on the M1 and AVX2 on the Xeon; the two are different machines and not comparable.
- **Kaggle uses 5 repetitions and reports no confidence intervals**; the Mac suites use 10 repetitions with 95% intervals (section 9), so Kaggle differences smaller than run-to-run noise cannot be judged.
- **Inter-city travel is CPU-only and uses assumed flows** (config/travel.toml, illustrative, not measured commuting data); GPU backends do not run the travel coupling and the effect found is small (section 10).
- **The calibration target is assumed** (a 5-day doubling time), not fitted to observed case data.
- **Mac vs Kaggle** are different hardware; cross-machine times are not like-for-like. Metal 10M results are not checked against sequential.
- **Mac test logs were not saved**; Mac golden-test status is taken from docs/PROGRESS.md. The Kaggle golden-table run panicked on the NEON row after 8 PASS rows (since fixed to skip NEON on non-aarch64, not re-run there).
- PTX analysis markdown files in results/ are unfilled templates; counts come from `summary.json`.
- Figures are rendered live in the Lab web app (not exported as images); this report references the tab for each.
